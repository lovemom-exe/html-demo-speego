From Barcodes to Receiving: How Inbound Fulfillment Works at SpeeGo

SpeeGo Editorial Team · September 17, 2026
Share: Facebook ↗ · LinkedIn ↗ · Copy link

A standardized receiving procedure is the backbone of seamless order fulfillment. When inbound cargo arrives with compliant barcodes and advance notification, warehouse receiving velocity increases drastically while inventory discrepancies drop to near zero.

1. Barcode Setup

Every individual SKU requires a unique, scannable barcode (UPC, EAN-13, or proprietary SKU code). Barcode labels must feature high-contrast print, placed visibly on each unit package as well as outer master cartons to facilitate rapid optical scanning.

2. Create an Inbound Request (IR)

Prior to dispatching cargo to the SpeeGo fulfillment hub, merchants create an Inbound Request (IR) in the portal. Key data inputs include SKU quantities, expected delivery dates (ETA), and packing manifests, enabling warehouse teams to optimize bin and pallet rack allocation.

3. Physical Receiving and Inspection

Upon dock arrival, the SpeeGo receiving team unloads goods, verifies physical piece counts against the Bill of Lading, inspects carton exterior conditions, and performs barcode test-scans. Any carton damages or quantity discrepancies are immediately documented with photo proof.

4. Put-away and Order Fulfillment

Once verified, inventory counts synchronize into the live WMS and stock is put away into designated picking locations. Units become immediately available for automated multi-channel order synchronization, pick-pack fulfillment, and final-mile carrier handover.
